Atomic Networks · Polymarket Behavioral Cohort Analysis · May 2026

The atomic networks of Polymarket — how traders actually cluster, and where the bonds form.

Behavioral community detection on 59.2M trader–market edges across Polymarket's full history (Oct 2020 – Jan 2026, 1.98M wallets, 300K markets) surfaces 18 networks of traders bonded by shared trading behavior. The bonds between networks (overlap in trader bases) reveal the actual audience structure of the platform. Time horizon splits crypto traders into five distinct networks on the same underlyings. Election eras are separate networks with measurable bonds across cycles. "Sports" splits into two. Polymarket's category tags don't see any of this — only behavior does.

Spotlight · Time horizon splits crypto traders into five networks

Five distinct clusters trade the same underlyings (BTC / ETH / SOL). They share some traders — adjacent horizons bond more strongly (15-min BTC ↔ hourly BTC, Jaccard 0.23). But the populations are mostly distinct.

Hover to peek · click for full breakdown
The interesting bonds inside: 15-min BTC (Cluster 4) ↔ Hourly BTC-recent (Cluster 5) share 29K traders at Jaccard 0.23 — the strongest in this subset. 15-min ETH/SOL (Cluster 3) is the most isolated. Cluster 10 (daily / price-target) is the largest by volume but has the broadest, most diffuse audience.

Spotlight · Election eras are separate networks with bonds across cycles

Three election-era clusters. The 2024-primary and 2024-general clusters share 569K traders (Jaccard 0.32) — the single strongest bond in the entire network. 2020 voters mostly didn't return for 2024 (much weaker bond). Each cycle creates a substantially fresh audience.

Showing 3 clusters · $21.6B total · ~1.86M distinct traders
Hover to peek · click for full breakdown
The interesting bonds inside: 2024 Dem primary ↔ 2024 general is the strongest bond in the entire network — 569K shared traders, Jaccard 0.32. By contrast, 2020 ↔ 2024 general only shares 25K traders (Jaccard 0.015) — basically two different generations of Polymarket users.

Spotlight · Inside Cluster 1 — the 2024 mega-cluster is a gradient, not tribes

Recursive Louvain on Cluster 1 ($17B, 24,956 markets) surfaces 4 internal sub-communities — but with weak separation (modularity 0.226). They're not distinct tribes, they're the same population at different intensities of engagement.

Inner structure of the largest cluster
The gradient runs: Headline whales ($8.9B in just 1,078 markets — Trump 2024, Harris 2024, inauguration) → News-cycle generalists ($5.4B across 12K markets — Zelenskyy's suit, TikTok ban, Russia/Ukraine, Epstein files) → International politics specialists ($1.6B — Korea/Romania/Poland presidentials) → Niche tourists ($1.1B — d4vd Google searches, NJ governor, Vietnam CCP). Modularity 0.226 is low because these aren't separate audiences — they're the same wallets at different attention budgets.

Spotlight · "Sports" is two distinct networks under one tag

Polymarket bundles all sports under one category — but the wallet base is split. NFL + League of Legends bettors share some traders with NBA + soccer + boxing bettors (Jaccard 0.18), but the bond is moderate, not dominant. Two distinct audiences hidden under one tag.

The interesting bonds: ~81K traders sit at the intersection of NFL+esports and NBA+soccer — overlap exists, but the modal trader bets in one or the other, not both. NFL+esports is also strongly bonded to the 2024 election cluster (237K shared) — the election-tourist audience also bets NFL.

Methodology

Pipeline + key decisions that made the analysis honest.

Dataset: Jon-Becker/prediction-market-analysis — full Polymarket trade history scraped from Polygon (Oct 2020 – Jan 2026). 59.2M trader–market edges after extracting OrderFilled and FPMM events.

Filtering: Excluded 2 protocol contracts (CTF Exchange + NegRisk CTF Exchange) that appear as maker/taker in events but aren't real traders. Dropped 201 dust spammers (mean trade < $1, traded 1000+ markets) and 5,342 ultra-generalists (1000+ markets, blurs community boundaries). Singletons (1 market only) contribute no pairs.

Projection: Trader–market bipartite → market–market via Jaccard on shared traders (threshold ≥ 5 shared traders, Jaccard ≥ 0.01). Unweighted, not volume-weighted — answers "where do traders co-locate" not "where does capital co-flow."

Clustering: Louvain via igraph, weighted by Jaccard. Modularity 0.7838 — > 0.6 is generally considered well-separated.

Bonds: For each cluster-pair, count traders who participated in both. 153 cluster-pairs measured; the network viz shows the top ~30 bonds. Recursive Louvain on Cluster 1 (the mega-cluster) surfaces 4 sub-communities with low modularity (0.226) — the gradient, not tribes, finding.
